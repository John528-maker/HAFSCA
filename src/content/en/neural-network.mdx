## What is it?

A **neural network** is a composition of maps. The smallest useful picture is an affine layer plus a nonlinearity $\varphi$:

$$
a=\varphi(wx+b).
$$

Stacking **identity** maps collapses to one affine map. The bends of $\varphi$ are what extra depth can buy. This lesson does **not** train a net in the browser.

## Why it matters

Everything earlier on this site is still true: a model is $f(x;\theta)$, a loss is $J$, a gradient is $\nabla J$. A net is the same objects composed. Without naming composition, “layers” sound like a different kind of mathematics.

## Intuition

One ReLU is a hinge. Two ReLUs can make a bump. A sigmoid squash saturates, so $\varphi'$ dies in the tails — that is the activations lab on the gradient page, reused as the mechanism of vanishing credit.

## Mathematics

If every $\varphi$ is identity, $a_L=w_L\cdots w_1 x+\ldots$ is still linear in $x$. The chain rule through a nonlinearity is

$$
\frac{\partial a_\ell}{\partial a_{\ell-1}}=\varphi'(z_\ell)\,w_\ell,
$$

the same recurrence the activations experiment already runs. Backpropagation is that idea applied to every weight, not a new loss.

## Summary

A net is composed functions. Depth without $\varphi$ is one matrix. Training a multilayer net in the browser is out of this MVP; the chain of $\varphi'$ is already interactive.
