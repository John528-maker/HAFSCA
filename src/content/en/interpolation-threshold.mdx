## What is it?

The **interpolation threshold** is the complexity where the number of parameters catches the number of **training** points:

$$
p=n_{\mathrm{train}}.
$$

For polynomials on this site, $p=d+1$, so the theoretical degree is $n_{\mathrm{train}}-1$. The flagship lab reports that formula. It does **not** hunt for a train-MSE cutoff.

## Why it matters

Below the threshold, least squares is typically overdetermined: you cannot hit every training $y$ exactly (unless the data happen to lie on the model). At and beyond it, interpolators exist. Double descent is a claim about test error **near** this crossing, not a second name for overfitting.

## Intuition

Think of $p$ knobs and $n$ numbers to match. When $p<n$, leftover training error is normal. When $p=n$ on a generic design, there is just enough freedom to interpolate. When $p>n$, many interpolators exist and the engine picks min-norm.

Interpolation of noisy labels is not “perfect learning.” Train MSE near $0$ can sit next to terrible test error. That peak is the next two lessons.

## Mathematics

Count parameters honestly: intercept plus degree $d$ is $d+1$ columns of $X$. The split is the one the experiment already uses. Changing `trainRatio` moves $n_{\mathrm{train}}$ and therefore moves the threshold.

A tiny relative ridge means train MSE is not literally machine zero, but the **reported** threshold stays $n_{\mathrm{train}}-1$ by definition.

## Summary

Interpolation is $p=n_{\mathrm{train}}$, degree $n_{\mathrm{train}}-1$ on this engine. It is a count, not a vibe.
