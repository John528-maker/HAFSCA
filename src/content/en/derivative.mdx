## What is it?

The **derivative** of a one-parameter loss $J(\theta)$ is the slope of the best local linear approximation:

$$
J'(\theta)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{J(\theta+h)-J(\theta)}{h}.
$$

If $J'(\theta)<0$, a tiny increase in $\theta$ **decreases** $J$. The derivative reports the slope. It does not, by itself, move $\theta$.

## Why it matters

Every later tool — partials, the gradient, gradient descent — is this one idea with more coordinates. Naming the slope first keeps “the computer found a fit” from sounding like magic.

## Intuition

Think of an exchange rate: nudge $\theta$ up by a hair, how much does $J$ change? Positive slope means “uphill if you increase $\theta$.”

## Mathematics

This site’s loss is $J(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_i(\theta x_i-y_i)^2$ for a one-parameter model $\hat y=\theta x$. Term $i$ has residual $u_i=\theta x_i-y_i$. Chain rule:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}u_i^2=2u_i\cdot x_i,
$$

so

$$
J'(\theta)=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\theta x_i-y_i)\,x_i.
$$

The extra $2$ is the same $2$ that makes $H_{22}=2$ on the two-parameter lab. There is still **no** $\tfrac12$ in $J$.

## Derivation check

$n=2$, $(x,y)=(1,3)$ and $(2,5)$. Then $J'(\theta)=5\theta-13$. At $\theta=2$, $J'(2)=-3$: increasing $\theta$ lowers the loss. The critical point $5\theta=13$ is $\theta^\star=2.6$, which is also the normal-equation answer $X^\top X=5$, $X^\top y=13$.

## Summary

A derivative is a local slope of $J$, with residual $\hat y-y$ and the site’s $1/n$ MSE.
