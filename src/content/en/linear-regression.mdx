# What is it?

**Linear regression** means the prediction is linear in the **parameters** $\theta$, not necessarily in $x$:

$$
\hat y = \theta^{\top}\phi(x).
$$

A straight line $\hat y = wx + b$ is the two-parameter case $\phi(x)=(1,x)^{\top}$. A polynomial in $x$ is still linear regression in $\theta$.

# Why it matters

The live labs on this site fit polynomials. They are linear models in Chebyshev features. The scatter lab below is the same criterion with $p=2$: unique intercept and slope that minimize mean squared *vertical* error.

# Intuition

Drag an endpoint. The line rotates more than when you drag a point near $\bar x$. That is leverage, not a drawing accident.

# Mathematics

Residual convention: $r_i=\hat y_i-y_i=wx_i+b-y_i$. Loss $J=\frac1n\sum r_i^2$ (no $\tfrac12$). If intercept is on and $S_{xx}=\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2>0$,

$$
w=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}},\qquad b=\bar y-w\bar x.
$$

If all $x$ values coincide, there is **no unique slope**. The lab checks $S_{xx}$ *before* calling the solver, so ridge cannot invent a fake line.

# Summary

If you remember one sentence: **linear in $\theta$, and the line is the vertical-MSE minimizer, not a freehand fit.**
