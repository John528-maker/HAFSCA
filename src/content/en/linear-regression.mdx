# What is it?

**Linear regression** means the prediction is linear in the **parameters** $\theta$, not necessarily in $x$:

$$
\hat y = \theta^{\top}\phi(x).
$$

A polynomial in $x$ is still linear regression in $\theta$.

# Why it matters

The live labs on this site fit polynomials. They are linear models in Chebyshev features $\phi(x)=(T_0(x),\ldots,T_d(x))^{\top}$. That is why a “wiggly” curve can still be ordinary least squares.

# Intuition

“Linear” is about how $\theta$ enters, not about the graph looking like a straight line.

# Mathematics

For $n$ training points stack the rows $\phi(x_i)^{\top}$ into $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}$. Then $\hat{\mathbf y}=X\theta$. Residual convention on this site: $\mathbf r=X\theta-\mathbf y$ (prediction minus target).

# Derivation

Mean squared error (no $\tfrac12$ factor, matching the engine) is

$$
J(\theta)=\frac{1}{n}\lVert X\theta-y\rVert_2^2.
$$

The gradient is $\nabla J(\theta)=\frac{2}{n}X^{\top}(X\theta-y)$. Setting it to zero gives the normal equations $X^{\top}X\hat\theta=X^{\top}y$.

# Summary

If you remember one sentence: **linear in $\theta$, not necessarily in $x$**.
